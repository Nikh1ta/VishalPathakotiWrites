The Whispers from the Charred Remains

Chapter 10 — The Door That Should Never Have Opened

Vishwa ran. He didn't know where he was going. He didn't know where Rudraksha was. He only knew one thing: something was behind him. Something fast. Something that didn't breathe. The basement corridor seemed endless. Vishwa's shoes slipped against the wet floor as he ran.

Behind him—

DUM. DUM. DUM.

The sound grew louder.

Vishwa looked over his shoulder.

Nothing.

He turned forward.

Nothing.

Then—a face appeared directly in front of him.

Vishwa screamed.

"Ayyayyo!"

He jumped backward.

The face disappeared.

Vishwa stared.

"Deyyam aa?"

Silence.

"Avi aa?"

A voice came from behind him.

"Rey!"

Vishwa turned.

Avi was standing there.

Vishwa nearly punched him.

"Arey!"

Avi raised both hands.

"Sorry ra! Nenu dhayyam kaadu."

Vishwa was breathing heavily.

"Ida em chesthunav..?!"

Avi looked around.

"Adhe nenu kuda nannu adugutunna question."

Vishwa stared at him.

Avi pointed toward the corridor.

"Actually, nenu paiki vellali anukunna."

Vishwa:

"Then?"

Avi:

"Wrong direction."

Vishwa looked at the darkness.

"How did you come here?"

Avi shrugged.

"Venakala dhayyalu tharumuthunte map choodataniki time undadu kada."

Vishwa closed his eyes.

"Avi..."

Avi:

"Hmm?"

"Nee venakala."

Avi froze.

His face slowly changed.

"Enti?"

Vishwa pointed.

Avi turned.

Nothing.

He looked back.

"Vishwa!"

Vishwa laughed.

Avi slapped his shoulder.

"Chi! Nee comedy ki time idena?"

Vishwa suddenly stopped laughing. Because behind Avi—something moved. A shadow. Tall. Thin. It disappeared around the corner.

Vishwa's smile vanished.

"Avi."

"Enti?"

"Parigettu."

Avi looked at him.

"Malli aa?"

Vishwa grabbed his arm.

"RUN!"

Both of them sprinted.

Voice Over (Scary, Suspenseful, and Playful):

Sometimes, the scariest ghosts are the ones that wait for punchlines before attacking. Sometimes, friendship is just running side by side into darkness—one of you cracking jokes, the other not laughing.

Above them, the remaining members of the Trek group were gathering.

Veera was counting heads.

"One, two, three..."

He stopped.

"Avi ekkada?"

Raj looked around.

"Basement lo undochu."

Everyone stared at him.

Veera blinked.

"Neekela telusu?"

Raj shrugged.

"Evvaru vellakoodadani cheppina chotike velthadu vaadu. English horror movies lo characters laaga."

Veera sighed.

"Correct."

Raj looked toward the basement stairs.

"Common sense ki holiday."

Rudraksha stood in front of the basement door. His face was pale. He knew something had changed. The protection around the building was weakening. The spirits were moving. The walls had begun cracking. One crack ran from the ceiling to the floor. Another appeared near the basement door. Rudraksha touched it. His hand trembled.

For twenty years, the door had remained sealed. For twenty years, Mayuri had been locked below. But now—the door was open. And someone had opened it from the inside.

Rudraksha whispered the name.

"Mayuri..."

The lights flickered.

A woman's voice came from below.

"Appa..."

Rudraksha froze.

His eyes widened.

His daughter.

After twenty years—she had spoken.

But it wasn't her voice alone.

Another voice whispered underneath it.

The Jungle Spirit.

Rudraksha stepped backward.

"Mayuri..."

The two voices merged.

"Appaaaa..."

Rudraksha fell against the wall.

Vishwa and Avi finally reached the staircase.

Vishwa saw Rudraksha.

"Sir!"

Rudraksha looked at them.

"Kindaki raavaddu."

Avi immediately stopped.

"Excellent."

Vishwa looked at him.

"Enti excellent?"

"First time somebody gave correct instructions."

Raj appeared behind them.

He looked into the basement.

"Too late."

Vishwa turned.

"Why?"

Raj pointed.

"Door already open."

The basement lights turned on.

One.

Two.

Three.

Then all of them went out.

Darkness.

A low growl filled the building.

Rudraksha closed his eyes. He understood. Mayuri was no longer alone. The Jungle Water Spirit had reached her. The two entities had merged. And the result was something far more powerful than either spirit alone.

The basement temperature dropped. Ten degrees colder. The walls cracked. The floor shook. Dust fell from the ceiling.

Avi whispered.

"Sir..."

Rudraksha looked at him.

"Enti?"

"Is there any chance manam idi repatki postpone cheyoch aa?"

Rudraksha stared.

Avi nodded.

"Just asking."

Raj looked at him.

"Ghost ki appointment eroje undi."

Avi frowned.

Avi: "Enti?"

Raj: "Repu varaku wait cheyyadu."

Avi stared at him.

"One-liner cheppava?"

Raj nodded.

"Avunu."

Avi: "Eesari timing baagundi."

Then—a woman stepped out of the darkness. Long hair. Bare feet. Her body appeared almost human. Almost.

Her head slowly lifted.

Vishwa couldn't breathe.

Rudraksha whispered—

"Mayuri."

The woman smiled.

But behind her—something else moved. A second shadow. The Jungle Spirit. Mayuri's body twisted. The two entities had become one.

Vishwa stepped backward.

"Rudraksha sir..."

Rudraksha raised his hand.

"Kadhalakandi."

Mayuri looked directly at Vishwa.

Then she smiled.

"Vishwa..."

Vishwa froze.

Avi whispered.

"Bro..."

"What?"

"Ghost ki nee name ela telusu?"

Vishwa stared at him.

"Naakem telusu!"

Avi nodded.

"Okay. Important question."

"Enti?"

"Na name kuda telusa?"

Vishwa turned.

"Avi!"

"Just checking!"

Mayuri suddenly screamed. The sound shook the entire building. Everyone covered their ears. Windows shattered. Lights exploded. The floor cracked.

Rudraksha shouted.

"RUN!"

Everyone ran.

Voice Over (Scary, Suspenseful):

When the dead call your name, running isn’t just the best option—it’s the only one. And as the Trek group fled through darkness and chaos, they realized: some doors should never be opened, and some spirits never awakened.
