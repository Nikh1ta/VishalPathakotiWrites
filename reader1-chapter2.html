The Whispers from the Charred Remains

Chapter 2 — The Wedding

The next morning began with chaos. The Trek Group had a simple plan: travel together, attend the wedding, enjoy the farmhouse, return home. Simple. Safe. Normal. Unfortunately, the group had Vishwa. Which meant nothing remained simple for long.

Voice Over (Scary, Suspenseful, and Playful):

The sun barely rose, but already there was shouting, cursing, and the unmistakable sound of someone losing a slipper. Somewhere in the shadows, even the crows seemed to laugh. If only the group knew—on this day, it wasn’t just chaos that would follow them.

By six in the morning, the group had already argued about luggage. By six-thirty, they had argued about breakfast. By seven, they had lost one person. By seven-fifteen, they discovered that the person they had lost was actually sitting inside the vehicle the entire time.

Veera opened the door.

"Srikanth!"

Srikanth looked up.

"Enti?"

"Memu ninnu twenty minutes nunchi search chestunnam!"

Srikanth blinked.

"Enduku?"

"Where were you?"

"Car lo."

Veera stared at him.

"Maatho?"

"Avunu."

"Since when?"

"Morning nunchi."

Everyone went silent.

Vishwa slowly looked at Veera.

"Bro..."

Veera turned.

"Cheppu."

"Manam safe ga trip complete cheyyalem."

"Enduku?"

Vishwa pointed at Srikanth.

"Vaadu car lo undi, manam vaadini search chestunnam."

Srikanth shrugged.

"Meeru correct ga search cheyyaledu."

Vishwa stared.

"Car lo kurchuni unnava."

"Avunu."

"Window pakkana kurchuni?"

"Avunu."

"Appudu memu ela miss ayyamu?"

Srikanth smiled.

"Peaceful ga Nature chusthunna."

Veera slapped his forehead.

"Maa peace paaduchesi Peaceful ga Nature anta!"

Voice Over (Scary, Suspenseful, and Playful):

Laughter echoed through the morning like a charm meant to ward off the unknown. They had no idea what was really hiding in the background—besides their own poor organizational skills. As wheels began to turn and music blared, nobody noticed the shadow that seemed just a little too dark on the side of the road.

That was how the journey began. The group traveled toward the wedding location, surrounded by jokes, music, food packets, and arguments over who controlled the playlist. Nobody noticed the strange dream Vishwa had experienced. Nobody knew that the destination they were traveling toward was already connected to something ancient. Something that had been waiting for them.

The wedding belonged to Nandu. The strange part was that Vishwa had somehow become involved in the story. It began with a fight. A man. An argument. And Vishwa's temper. Vishwa had always distrusted people. That wasn't something he explained easily. Whenever someone asked him about it, he changed the subject. Whenever someone got too close, he pulled away. And when someone tried to joke about relationships—Vishwa became strangely quiet. Vaishnavi had noticed. She had noticed long before the others. But she never forced him to talk. Until that trip. Until the Jungle Stay. Until the night when everything changed.

For now, however, Vishwa was simply Vishwa. He was sitting in the vehicle, holding a bottle. Again.

Vaishnavi looked at it.

"Vishwa, malli na?"

Vishwa looked at the bottle.

"Avunu."

"Morning breakfast mundu?"

"Avunu."

"Ninna?"

"Avunu."

"Monna?"

Vishwa paused.

"Monna kuda."

Vaishnavi stared at him.

"Vishwa, padnalugu rojula nunchi daily drink chestunnav."

Vishwa calmly took another sip.

"Consistency."

Veera looked back from the front.

"Bro, consistency kaadu ra, medical emergency."

Vishwa ignored him.

Vaishnavi shook her head.

"At least oka roju aina vadiley."

Vishwa looked out the window.

"People ni nammalenu. Idi aina naa control lo undi."

Voice Over (Scary, Suspenseful, and Playful):

The laughter faded, as if someone had turned down the volume on life. For a moment, only the sound of the road remained. Even the jungle outside seemed to lean in, listening, waiting for secrets to slip out when nobody was watching.

The laughter disappeared. For a second, nobody spoke. Vaishnavi looked at him. She knew there was truth behind it. Vishwa didn't trust people. Not anymore. And that was when his fourteen-day drinking phase stopped being just a joke. It had become his way of avoiding memories. Avoiding questions. Avoiding Ananya. Avoiding himself.

Veera slowly spoke.

"Bro..."

Vishwa didn't look at him.

"Enti?"

"Mandhu ni friend laga treat cheyyaku."

Vishwa gave a small smile.

"Friend aina betray chestadu, mandhu cheyadhu."

The vehicle became quiet.

Then Avi, sitting in the back, suddenly raised his hand.

"One clarification."

Everyone looked at him.

"Enti?"

Avi pointed at Vishwa's bottle.

"Adi friend aa enemy aa?"

Vishwa frowned.

"Enduku?"

Avi shrugged.

"Daily kalusthunnav kada. Relationship status telusukundam ani."

The group exploded with laughter.

Vishwa threw a pillow at him. Avi ducked.

"Rey! Nenu serious ga adiga!"

Raj looked up from his phone.

"Fourteen days drinking."

He paused.

"Fifteenth day ki bottle kuda Vishwa ni block chestundi."

The group exploded with laughter.

Vishwa shook his head.

"Meeru andaroo useless."

Avi grinned.

"At least sober ga useless kaadu."

Vishwa stared at him.

"Avi."

"Hmm?"

"Shut up."

"Okay."

Two seconds passed.

"Actually—"

"Avi!"

"Okay, okay!"

The laughter continued. But behind the jokes—Vishwa's hand tightened around the bottle. Because the truth was simple. He wasn't drinking because he enjoyed it. He was drinking because remembering was worse. And somewhere in the back of his mind—Ananya's face still remained.

Voice Over (Scary, Suspenseful, and Playful):

The jokes bounced around like flashlights in the dark, keeping real fears at bay. But shadows have a sneaky way of waiting for laughter to fade before creeping closer. In the brief silences, the road seemed a little colder, the forest a little thicker.

The wedding location was unlike anything they expected. The farmhouse stood deep inside a jungle. No nearby houses. No city lights. No traffic. Only trees. Fog. Rain. And a massive infinity pool overlooking the forest. The entire place looked unreal. The rain fell continuously. The sky was gray. The jungle disappeared into mist.

Vishwa stood near the pool, looking around. Something about the place bothered him. He couldn't explain why.

Vaishnavi walked beside him.

"Em ayyindi?"

Vishwa shook his head.

"Em ledu."

"Face chusthe something undi."

"Na face alane untundi."

Vaishnavi smiled.

"Adi kuda nijame."

Vishwa gave her an irritated look.

"Thanks."

"Welcome."

The group laughed. But Vishwa's eyes remained fixed on the jungle. For one brief moment—he thought he saw someone standing between the trees. A woman. Motionless. Watching.

Vishwa blinked.

Gone.

"Vishwa?"

He turned. Vaishnavi was standing beside him.

"Enti?"

"Nuvvu ekkada chustunnav?"

"Em ledu."

Vaishnavi looked toward the jungle. Nothing was there.

"Over imagination."

"Maybe."

Then Vaishnavi smiled.

"Raa."

"Ekadiki?"

"Wedding ki."

Vishwa sighed.

"Life lo peace ane concept ledu."

Vaishnavi grabbed his hand and pulled him toward the others. Vishwa allowed himself to be dragged.

Voice Over (Scary, Suspenseful, and Playful):

Behind them, the jungle remained silent—almost too silent. If anyone had looked back, maybe they’d have seen the leaves shiver or a shadow slip between the trees. But nobody did. Not yet. For now, the group laughed, celebrated, and raced toward the farmhouse—completely unaware that they were not the only ones waiting for the ceremony to begin.
