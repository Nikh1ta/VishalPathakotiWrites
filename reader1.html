The Whispers from the Charred Remains

Chapter 1 — The Dream

There are dreams that disappear the moment you open your eyes. Then there are dreams that stay. They sit somewhere behind your eyes, waiting. Waiting for the right night. Waiting for the right place. Waiting for you to realize that what you thought was a dream was actually a warning. Vishwa had always believed dreams were meaningless. At least, that was what he told everyone. The problem was that Vishwa also had a habit of saying things with complete confidence even when he had absolutely no idea what he was talking about.

That particular night, Vishwa dreamed of a forest. Not an ordinary forest. The trees were impossibly tall, their branches twisting together like the fingers of something enormous. Thick fog covered the ground. Rain fell silently, though Vishwa could see no clouds. In front of him stood a black stone. It was shaped like a small shrine. A strange blue pendant rested on top of it. Vishwa stepped forward. The moment his fingers touched the pendant—a woman's voice whispered from somewhere behind him, "Vishwa..." He froze. The voice sounded familiar. Too familiar. He slowly turned. Nothing. Only fog. Then a second voice came. This one was deeper. Older. Angrier: "Ee talupu teravaku."

Vishwa's heart began pounding.

"Ye talupu?"

Silence.

Then—a wooden door appeared in the middle of the forest. It was old. Burned. Covered in black marks. Something was scratching from the other side.

Scratch. Scratch. Scratch.

Vishwa stepped backward. The scratching stopped. A woman's fingers slowly appeared beneath the door. They were long. Too long. The nails were black. Then a voice whispered, "Teruvu."

Vishwa turned and ran. The forest changed around him. Trees moved. The fog thickened. The ground disappeared beneath his feet. He fell. And just before he hit the ground—Vishwa woke up.

He sat upright in bed. His breathing was heavy. For several seconds, he didn't move. Then he looked around. His room was normal. His fan was spinning. His phone was charging. His blanket was on the floor. Everything was fine.

Vishwa wiped sweat from his forehead. "Dream aa idi."

He looked at the clock. 3:03 AM.

Vishwa stared at the time. Then he slowly pulled the blanket over his head.

"Repat nunchi drinking tagginchali."

He paused.

"Actually... repatnundi life change."

A moment later, his phone vibrated. Vishwa screamed. He screamed. He grabbed the phone. It was only a group message.

Trek GROUP — 87 unread messages.

"87 messages aa?"

He opened the chat. The messages were mostly from Veera.

Veera: "Rey andharu Lechara?"

Veera: "Repu wedding confirm ey ga..."

Veera: "andharu osthunaru kadha?"

Veera: "Especially Vishwa."

Vishwa immediately typed.

"Nenu endhuku raanu..."

Veera replied instantly.

"Nuv chesi a pani valla ne ga ee Pelli ayyedhi."

Vishwa frowned.

"Adi situation atla ayindhi appudu."

Srikanth replied.

"Nee situation enti appudu?"

Vishwa stared at the phone.

"Naaku kuda exact ga teliyadu..."

Vaishnavi sent another message.

"Repu 6 ki ready ga undandi."

Vishwa sat upright.

"Morning six aa?"

Veera replied.

"Avunu Mari location emaina inti pakkana ankunava..."

Vishwa typed.

"Six ante morning six aa?"

Veera replied.

"Kaadu ra, evening six."

Vishwa smiled.

Three seconds later—Veera sent another message.

"Obviously morning six."

Vishwa threw the phone onto the bed.

"Friends antaru... parents la behave chestharu."

He lay back down. But sleep didn't come easily. Because somewhere deep inside his mind—the dream was still waiting. And somewhere far away, beyond the city lights, something else had already begun waiting for them.

HOW THEY MET

A Story of the Trek Group

Voice Over (Warm, friendly):

Some groups are born in classrooms. Others at work. But this group? They were born on a trek—with nine strangers, nine different backgrounds, and one master plan: survive the wild... or at least, each other.

Vaishnavi was the mastermind behind it all. She believed friendships shouldn’t be scheduled—they should just happen. In her crew were Vishwa, Vaishnavi, and Nandu—the Three Musketeers of college. Where one went, the other two followed, usually into trouble.

Then came Srinivas and Hansika. College mates, always teasing, always denying. They’d say "just friends" so much, even Google started suggesting it as autofill. Yet, after the trek, they finally confessed. Fast-forward: wedding bells!

Veera and Raj were childhood buddies from Kakinada. Veera lived inside Excel sheets and meetings, but Raj had a one-step solution. "Nature unna place ki veldham." Before Veera could protest, he was already halfway up a hill.

Srikanth and Avi? Local pals. Avi was so bored with life, he’d started rating biscuits for fun. Srikanth rescued him: "Raa... konchem diversion la untundi." Avi agreed, not realizing his life was about to become a full-time thriller.

Voice Over (Smiling):

The first day was classic awkward—fake smiles, forced intros, nine people, nine strangers. But Day Two? Rain trapped them in their tents. Someone brought out cards. Stories took over. Embarrassing college tales became viral. By midnight, even ghost stories had made their rounds. The strangers? Not so strange now.

Day Three: They hit the trekking peak just before sunrise. Clouds floated below them, the entire valley hidden under mist. Someone yelled for a group photo. They all squeezed in. Click! A memory, a promise, and a beginning they didn’t recognize yet.

Before heading down, Vaishnavi grinned: "Prati six months ki oka trip... pakka eldham."

Everyone’s hands shot up. Promise made. Spoiler alert: that promise was about to become a lifeline.

The trek ended, but the friendship didn’t. The WhatsApp group was never silent—calls, birthdays, weekend meet-ups, memes, and never-ending teasing. Without realizing, nine strangers had become a family. They thought nothing could break them. Fate, meanwhile, was already preparing the test.

Voice Over (Playful):

A few days post-trek, the gang met up in Hyderabad. By then, Srinivas had proposed to Hansika and their wedding was on. The group? Buzzing.

Vaishnavi, ever the planner, lined everyone up at the laser tag arena. "Okay... teams are ready!"

She pointed to Team Alpha: Veera, Vaishnavi, Srinivas, and Raj. Then to Team Bravo: Vishwa, Nandu, Avi, Srikanth, Hansika.

Avi: "One minute... vallaki naluguru... manam aiduguru."

Veera (grinning): "Meeru numbers lo strong... mem strategy lo strong."

Raj, straight-faced: "Or maybe... meeru survive avvadam kosam extra player."

The referee: "Three... Two... One... START!"

Arena goes dark, lasers everywhere.

Vaishnavi immediately barked orders: "Srinivas, left side cover! Veera, center! Raj, right flank!"

Team Alpha moved like a SWAT team.

Team Bravo? Pure chaos.

Avi ran the wrong way: "Enemy ekkada?"

Srikanth: "Mundu!"

Avi: "Ee mundu?"

BEEP!

Out already.

"Already aa?"

The group cackled over the speakers.

Vishwa and Nandu hid behind a barricade.

Vishwa: "I'll distract them."

Nandu: "Okay."

Vishwa jumps out: "Attack—"

BEEP! BEEP!

Two seconds, double elimination.

He checked the scoreboard: "Strategy fail."

Vaishnavi (laughing): "Cinemalu ekkuva chusthunattu unnav."

Vishwa: "Practice round idhi. Next round chudu icchipadestha."

Meanwhile, Veera and Srinivas were like clockwork—Veera distracted, Srinivas scored. Raj was the ninja. Team Bravo members dropped like flies until only Hansika remained.

"One against four?"

Vaishnavi, eyes glinting: "Challenge accepted."

Hansika dodged, slid, tagged Srinivas.

BEEP!

"Sorry, future husband!"

Srinivas: "Em undhi le inka... kaani alvat cheskunta."

Laugh riot.

Final showdown: Vaishnavi, Veera, Raj vs. Hansika.

Hansika got Veera with a surprise shot, but Vaishnavi and Raj fired together.

BEEP! GAME OVER. WINNERS – TEAM ALPHA!

Arena erupted.

Avi, removing his helmet with fake drama: "I want THIRD EMPIRE."

Vishwa: "Idhi cricket kaadu ra."

Avi, arms folded: "Then rematch."

Vaishnavi, holding the trophy: "Next game... no excuses."

Voice Over (Gentle, nostalgic):

A month before Srinivas and Hansika’s wedding, the whole group gathered. Pre-wedding, wedding—honestly, who cared? The point was to make memories.

Vishwa (grabbing a soda and looking at the cake): "Idi pre-wedding party aa, lekapote Srinivas ni roast cheyyadaniki excuse aa?"

Vaishnavi: "Difference enti?"

Vishwa: "Inthakmundhu ante Hansika appud appud roast chesedhi Srinivas ni ippat nundi roju adhe untadhi."

Group: laughs, snacks spill everywhere

Vaishnavi rolled her eyes. "Adhi tharvatha vishayam but ippud Party ki roast compulsory, Vishwa."

Right then, Veera and Raj walked in with backpacks.

Avi: "Kakinada batch entry!"

Veera, arms wide: "Hyderabad... mem vachesammm."

Raj, soft smile: "Actually Hyderabad kosam kaadu... mee andarini kalavadaniki."

Vishwa: "Baane..."

Voice Over (Warm):

Ever since that trek, Veera and Raj made it a habit to visit Hyderabad—birthdays, dinners, movies, or just because. Distance? Irrelevant. This was family now.

Srinivas (hugging Veera): "Rey... wedding preparations kanna meeru ostharu raaro ani tension ekva unde naaku."

Veera: "Wedding miss avtham anukunnava?"

Srinivas: "Meeru late ayyaru kada, so inka raaru ankunna."

Raj: "Kakinada traffic valla late ayyam, maa thappu kaadhu."

Avi (blinking): "Kakinada lo traffic untunda?"

Raj: "Untundi."

Avi: "Ekada?"

Raj: "Tea shop daggara untadhi... mana Veera ki fresh air kavali ga, akkada traffic jam ayindhi."

Veera: "Cheppa ra, inka washroom la traffic jam, hotel lo traffic jam, okka ammai kanpichi traffic jam ani."

Group: erupts into cackles

Hansika arrived in a blue outfit, Nandu and Vaishnavi right behind. Vaishnavi clapped: "Okay everyone! Let's start!"

That night was a blur of music, dance, and laughter—memories they'd carry forever.

Voice Over (Soft, heartfelt):

One month later, Hansika and Srinivas tied the knot at a beautiful venue. Promises, tears, hugs. The Trek Group watched, hearts full. Some bonds don’t need blood—just one trek and a lifetime of friendship.

Everyone gathered for a group selfie—laughing, teasing, reliving every silly moment.

Vishwa (to the selfie group): "Selfie ki filter vadakunda original ga pettandi please. Nenu inka single unta, future ki evidence undali."

None of them knew... this would be one of the last times they’d laugh without fear.
